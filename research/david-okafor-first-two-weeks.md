# What David Okafor Needs From Josh in the First Two Weeks

## Where David actually is

Not resistant. Not yet a champion. Uncertain, under-resourced to figure this out alone, and personally invested (he's tried prompting himself, with mixed results) without having landed on something that worked. He hired Caylent because the mandate is board-level and he doesn't have the bandwidth to self-diagnose where to start. [Source: scenario-brief.pdf]

## What he needs, specifically

1. **Josh doing the discovery legwork *for* him, not asking him to produce it.** David said himself he doesn't have the bandwidth — handing him a discovery template to fill out would just recreate the problem that got Caylent hired. Josh runs the kickoff session; David shows up and talks about his work.
2. **A fast, low-risk, visible win.** Something concrete he can point to with the PE sponsor inside two weeks — not a roadmap, not a framework, an actual artifact (e.g., a first-draft variance explanation produced faster, that he still fully stands behind after editing it).
3. **Language to describe the program in board terms.** David's problem isn't just "make AI work" — it's "be able to say something true and confident about it at the next board touchpoint." Part of the deliverable is the sentence he gets to say, not just the pilot itself.
4. **Explicit permission and cover for his analysts to experiment.** If this reads as productivity-policing, it fails regardless of the tooling. David needs Josh to frame this to the team in a way David can stand behind — psychological safety for the two analysts on the kickoff call is part of the deliverable, not a side effect.
5. **A trust model he doesn't have to invent himself.** David shouldn't be the one improvising "how do we know the AI didn't get it wrong" — that answer (draft-then-verify, judgment stays with the analyst) needs to already be in Josh's opening framing, so David isn't defending an unproven idea to his own team.

## What "good" looks like at the two-week mark

- David has been in the room for a live demonstration on his team's actual work, not a slide deck (see `discovery/discovery-session-design.md`)
- A lighthouse use case is chosen and named, with David's buy-in, not handed to him
- David has a one-paragraph version of "here's what we're doing and why" he could say to the PE sponsor without notes
- At least one analyst has seen their own recent task done differently and reacted to it directly — David doesn't have to sell it secondhand

## What Josh should not do in these two weeks

- Present a menu of AI use cases and ask David to prioritize — he doesn't have the context or bandwidth to do that well yet, and it would just relocate the uncertainty back onto him
- Lead with tooling, licenses, or capability talk — none of that is what "uncertain, not resistant" is asking for
- Treat the kickoff call as a listening exercise only — David needs to leave it with something, not just have been heard

## Framing to open with

- **Validation first.** He got a one-hour orientation and no workflow-specific toolkit, on a team with no slack, so stalling out makes sense. Bringing in help is the right call, not an admission of failure.
- **Proof from peers.** Show real finance examples before any plan, including Anthropic's own finance team, which uses Claude for variance analysis and first-pass commentary (see `anthropic-finance-evidence.md`).

## What we ask of David at kickoff

These are decisions only he can make. Everything else is ours.

1. **Champions.** Are the two analysts on the call the right ones? Is anyone else a better fit?
2. **Interviews.** Which analysts should we talk to, and is any team at Meridian already using Claude well?
3. **Discovery session.** Who should be in the room: all 8, or a core group?
4. **Timing.** When do close and board prep peak? When is a workshop safe to hold?
5. **Ways of working.** Check-in cadence, channel, and who makes the call.
6. **Artifacts and access.** Last variance pack, templates, data-handling rules, and Claude Enterprise usage data (the fastest answer to "is this org-wide?").

He leaves kickoff with a one-page 30-day plan with dates, named champions, and the next meetings booked.
