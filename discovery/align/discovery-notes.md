# Discovery Notes — Synthesized Themes

Three themes, each labeled by confidence. These are what `deliverables/ai-relevance-position.md` and `discovery-session-design.md` are built on.

## 1. The work is invisible because it's constant (`hypothesis — validate`)

Nobody asked this team "what's slow or annoying about your week" and got "nothing" as an honest answer to a question about a workload that's *always* been there. Standard discovery questions ("what's your biggest pain point?") fail against work that's never been anything other than manual — there's no before/after contrast to notice. The fix isn't asking about pain; it's asking about time and judgment on a specific, recent, real task.

## 2. Orientation ≠ enablement (cited + inference)

A 1-hour mandatory session covering a general-purpose tool [Source: scenario-brief.pdf] doesn't answer "how does this apply to *my* variance write-up." Adoption tools that stop at capability demos, without a task-specific worked example, routinely produce exactly this outcome — near-zero usage despite genuine license spend. `hypothesis — validate`: confirm at kickoff whether the orientation included any FP&A-specific example, or was purely generic.

## 3. Uncertainty reads as disinterest, but isn't (cited)

David has tried prompts himself with mixed results and is "genuinely uncertain about where to start" [Source: scenario-brief.pdf] — this is a bandwidth and specificity problem, not a buy-in problem. The team's "this doesn't apply to us" isn't obstruction; it's an honest report that nobody has yet shown them the specific place it applies. That distinction is the difference between an adoption-campaign engagement and a workflow-design engagement — this one is the latter.
