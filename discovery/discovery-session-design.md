# Designing a Discovery Session That Actually Lands

This answers the brief's hardest ask directly: *this team can't see their own pain because the workload has always existed. How do you surface it, and how do you design a session that creates a "this is actually relevant to me" moment?*

## Why the standard approach fails here

Asking "what's your biggest pain point?" to someone who has never known their job any other way gets an honest "nothing, this is just the job." There's no before/after contrast for them to notice — the workload isn't a deviation from normal, it *is* normal. A session built around pain-point questions will produce the same "I don't see how this applies to us" the brief already told us to expect.

## The reframe

Don't ask about pain. Ask about **time** and **judgment**, on one specific, recent, real task. "Walk me through the last variance write-up you did, minute by minute" surfaces the manual reconciliation, formatting, and narrative-drafting labor that's invisible until it's named step by step. People can't self-report friction in the abstract, but they can narrate a task they just did.

## The mechanism that actually creates the moment

A slide about AI capabilities does not produce "oh, that's actually relevant to me." A **live demonstration on their own recently-completed task** does. The session's center of gravity should be: take the walkthrough from the question above, and — in the room, live — show a draft-then-verify pass on one piece of it (e.g., a first-pass variance explanation from the numbers they just described), then hand it back for them to judge, correct, and own. The moment isn't "AI can do X." It's "I just watched it do the thing I did Tuesday, and I still made the call on whether it was right."

## Session agenda (informed by `.claude/skills/interview-guide`'s structure)

| Segment | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | 5 min | Rapport, set expectations: this is discovery, not a pitch |
| Context | 10 min | David's situation specifically — orientation, his own prompt attempts, what the PE sponsor sees |
| Core (workflow walkthrough) | 20 min | The minute-by-minute walkthrough of a real recent task — the actual discovery engine |
| Live moment | 15 min | Draft-then-verify demo on a piece of what was just described, using their own numbers/template where possible |
| Solution exploration | 10 min | What would need to be true for them to trust this in practice; what they'd want to keep doing themselves |
| Closing | 5 min | What "going somewhere" looks like to them by two weeks from now |

Full question bank: `align/discovery-questions.md`.

## Where this differs for David vs. the analysts

- **David** owns the board narrative and cares about defensibility and not looking like he outsourced judgment to a tool. His "relevant to me" moment is seeing a board-narrative draft he can still fully stand behind after editing it — not a productivity claim.
- **Analysts** own the mechanical reconciliation and variance work day to day. Their "relevant to me" moment is seeing their own recent task done faster without losing the part where they catch the thing a template would have missed.

Design the live moment around whichever task the room actually brought — don't pre-script it further than that.
