# Company Profile — Meridian Capital Services

**Source:** `scenario-brief.pdf` (example scenario). All facts below are as given in the brief, not independently validated.

## Who they are

- Investment management services firm
- $195M revenue, 380 employees
- Acquired by a PE sponsor 11 months ago
- The acquisition's value-creation thesis explicitly includes an **"AI-enabled operations"** mandate — board-level, definition of success still open

## The team in scope

**FP&A (Financial Planning & Analysis)** — 8 analysts, owns:
- Monthly close
- Budget-vs-actuals reconciliation
- Variance analysis
- Management reporting
- The quarterly board narrative

The brief is explicit that the cadence of close, quarterly board prep, and management reporting "leaves little slack" — this team is busy by design, not understaffed by accident.

## What's happened so far

- 6 months ago: Claude enterprise licenses purchased org-wide
- Shortly after: a mandatory 1-hour orientation
- Since then: adoption has been **near zero**

The brief is explicit that this is *not* a story of people who tried and failed. The team's own framing: *"I just don't see how it applies to what we do. Our work is numbers-based and requires judgment."*

## Why Caylent is here

The PE sponsor made "AI-enabled finance function" a board-level initiative. David Okafor (Director of FP&A) hired Caylent because he needs to make progress on it and doesn't have the bandwidth to figure out where to start on his own. There's a kickoff call with David and two of his analysts scheduled.

## What this changes about the approach

See `deliverables/ai-relevance-position.md` for the full argument, but in short: "AI isn't relevant to our work" reads as a misread of the *tool* (generic chat prompting doesn't fit judgment-heavy, numbers-based work), not a misread of the *team's* capacity to benefit. That distinction is why the approach has to be workflow-embedded and demonstrated on a real task, not pitched as a capability.
